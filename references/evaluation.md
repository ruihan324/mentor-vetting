# Research-assessment standards and eight investigative dimensions

This reference extends `SKILL.md`. Read it when investigating a specific researcher. Preserve each of the eight dimensions; do not convert methodological advice into unsupported biographical claims.

### 3.4 Research capability vs achievements: qualitative assessment (mandatory)

**Research capability** concerns current ability to originate meaningful questions, design sound studies, execute and validate work, and sustain a research programme. **Research achievements** concern discoveries, methods, data, software, publications, patents, and independently demonstrated scholarly or practical impact. Neither is synonymous with **graduate mentoring capability**.

Use informed, field-sensitive **qualitative assessment as primary evidence** and verified quantitative indicators as context. Draw on DORA, the Leiden Manifesto, CoARA, and the Hong Kong Principles:

1. **Substantive quality of representative work.** Select **3–5 verified representative outputs** by default, or all if fewer are established. For each explain the research question, methods, findings, originality, limitations, and the researcher's actual contribution. Do not substitute journal prestige or JIF for content review.
2. **Original contribution and independence.** Check first/corresponding authorship, contribution statements, independent PI/co-PI roles, continuity of the research question, and relationships to former mentors or external collaborators. Neither author order nor collaboration count alone establishes intellectual leadership.
3. **Continuity and current capability.** Review a **year-by-year five-year output overview**, separating original research, reviews, conference papers, software/datasets, and patents. Test whether the researcher continues to lead projects. If incomplete, label it **"Verified selected works — not a complete output list"** rather than pretending a handful of papers constitutes a full annual inventory.
4. **Academic and real-world impact.** Seek evidence of independent reuse, replication, inclusion in guidelines, translation, products, or follow-on research. Use citation counts, h-index, JIF, and quartiles only as contextual measures; adjust comparisons for field and publication age.
5. **Methods and research integrity.** Consider design quality, controls, samples/statistics, transparency, code/data accessibility, reproducibility, and documented corrections/retractions. Unpublished data are not evidence of misconduct.
6. **Independence of funding and resources.** Differentiate PI, co-PI, and participant roles, competitive funding history, continuation, and purpose. Separate project totals, team support, and student scholarships.
7. **Training and academic service.** Record student research contributions, co-supervision, career support, peer review, scientific infrastructure, and service separately; overall mentoring quality must still be assessed through Dimension 3 and other relevant dimensions.

**Minimum representative-work analysis record** (mark unavailable fields):

`Full title/year/DOI | Research question | Core methods | Specific findings | This researcher's verified contribution and its evidence | Scientific/technical significance | Key limitations | Source`

If only the abstract was read, explicitly state **"Preliminary abstract-based analysis."** Never claim a comprehensive methodological assessment without the full paper. For substantial allegations examine the original manuscript, supporting data, and official notices as feasible; otherwise note that specialist review is required.

**Completeness terminology:** Only call an inventory a **"five-year publication list"** when identity attribution, multi-database searching, deduplication, preprint/final-publication matching, and year-by-year checks were completed. Otherwise write **"Verified selected works (not exhaustive)"** and identify what was and was not searched.

**Honest report status:** Covering eight dimensions means only that all eight were **attempted**, not that all were verified. If representative works were not reviewed thoroughly, the five-year search is incomplete, or current grants/supervision outcomes lack direct evidence, mark the executive summary **"Preliminary public-information vetting; key facts remain unverified."** Use **"In-depth public-record verification"** only after important identity/admissions, principal works, and funding claims have been independently cross-checked. Private lab experiences may remain explicitly pending, not silently presumed confirmed.

Guidance sources: DORA (https://sfdora.org/read/), Leiden Manifesto (https://www.aalto.fi/en/open-science-and-research/leiden-manifesto), CoARA (https://www.coara.org/agreement/the-commitments/), Hong Kong Principles (https://www.wcri2028.org/guidance/hong-kong-principles). Check current links when researching.

### 3.5 Field- and career-stage-matched comparisons (mandatory when comparing)

Before a comparative claim, identify the **research subfield, core methods over the last five years, career stage, principal appointment type, and comparison window**. Prefer **three to five comparable researchers**. Check each one's original-research proportion, representative work, PI/co-PI contribution, funding, and impact; note years since appointment and teaching/research loads.

**Never rank an applied food-technology professor against a structural virologist by paper count, JIF, or h-index alone.** When defensible peers cannot be identified, state **"Insufficient comparable peers; no ranking performed."** Explain why any chosen peers are comparable, sources used, meaningful differences, and limitations. Do not select deliberately weak/strong comparators or fabricate field percentiles.

## 4. The eight investigative dimensions

**Every dimension must be investigated internally**, with findings (or a documented search gap), interpretation, evidence and limitations mapped to an appropriate natural CV section. The report MUST contain each of the eight topic headings exactly once as ordinary peer-level Academic CV sections, not a standalone eight-dimension recap. For insufficient evidence write a short, explicit uncertainty line in that topic. The `Dimension` labels here guide investigation but are not the headings themselves.

### Dimension 1 — Research output and publication quality

**Assessment criteria**

- Five-year trends in papers, preprints, reviews, conference contributions; activity in the latest three years; substantive questions, methods, originality, and significance of representative work.
- Contributions as independent PI, corresponding/co-corresponding author, first author, or collaborator, interpreted through field conventions.
- Continued opportunities for student first/co-first authorship and fair attribution; never infer mentoring or intellectual independence from author order alone.
- Citations, h-index, i10-index, field-specific impact, open data/code, replicability, patents, translation, and other relevant outputs.
- Independence of collaborative networks, dependence on a single external team, documented corrections, retractions, and context.

**Sources:** For Mainland China: CNKI, Wanfang, Web of Science, Scopus, and publisher pages. For the other five countries: Google Scholar, PubMed, OpenAlex, Web of Science/Scopus, ORCID, publishers, and lab pages. Across regions: Crossmark, PubPeer, and Retraction Watch.

**Mandatory output:** Deep analysis of selected works using the Section 3.4 minimum record: full title, question, methods, findings, originality, this individual's contribution, evidence, and limitations. A year–journal–author-role list is insufficient. Explain specific publications and roles when asked about *Nature*, SCI indexing, or journal quartiles rather than treating a brand as scientific proof.

**Warning signs:** unexplained sustained output collapse, continuing inability to demonstrate independent contribution, repeated verified authorship disputes, substantiated integrity concerns, and disconnected shifts without a coherent programme. **No top-tier journal or public Google Scholar profile is not automatically a problem.**

### Dimension 2 — Grants, funding, and research resources

**Assessment criteria**

- Active awards, PI/co-PI/participant status, grant identifiers, dates, publicly reported value, status, renewal.
- Distinguish **research grants, institutional team support, equipment/start-up funds, student scholarships, salary/RA/TA support, and genuinely discretionary funds**; never conflate them.
- Actual equipment, consumables, computational resources, technical staff, core facilities, collaborators, and project feasibility.
- Tuition, stipend/salary, taxation or insurance when applicable, term of support, conditions, TA/RA workload, and local cost of living. Verify written-offer conditions when relevant.
- Mainland China: examine vertical (publicly awarded) vs horizontal (industry/contract) projects and their relationship to student's publishable degree work. Industry work offers opportunities but may also displace independent projects; do not prejudge.

**Country-specific:** Hard-money/soft-money arrangements generally concern salary security, not automatic student funding. In the US verify departmental guarantees, RA/TA and post-rotation arrangements. In the UK verify studentships, international tuition differences, and terms. In Canada verify guaranteed funding and TA/RA components. In Australia distinguish RTP fee offsets, stipends, and allowances awarded/managed by universities. In New Zealand distinguish university doctoral scholarships, PI projects, and international doctoral tuition rules.

**Sources:** Mainland China: NSFC, official state/provincial awards, LetPub as a lead, institutional research offices. US: NIH RePORTER, NSF Award Search and applicable funders. UK: UKRI/Gateway to Research and charities. Canada: NSERC/CIHR/SSHRC and universities. Australia: ARC/NHMRC and official RTP policies. New Zealand: MBIE, Marsden/Royal Society Te Apārangi, HRC, and universities. Trace funding acknowledgments to the underlying grant record.

**Minimum grant-table fields:** `Project | Funder | Researcher's role | Publicly stated amount and nature | Start/end | Relevance to proposed project | Source | Unresolved questions`.

**Warning signs:** inability to explain actual financial support, funding ending before expected completion without a plan, unclear fees/stipends, merely verbal promises, missing essential facilities, or unreasonable transfer of project costs to students. **No public grant record does not establish absence of funding.**

### Dimension 3 — Student supervision and outcomes

**Assessment criteria**

- **First verify formal eligibility:** ability to recruit and supervise the intended degree level at the relevant institution/programme; primary vs co-supervisor role. A senior title does not automatically confer graduate admissions authority.
- Mainland China: master's/doctoral admissions lists, postgraduate-school catalogues, formal joint-training arrangements. Do not assume an applied/vocational undergraduate institution awards PhDs merely because a professor holds a doctorate.
- Other covered countries: investigate actual programme admissions and supervision rules. Do not translate Mainland China's formal doctoral-supervisor title directly into a foreign appointment requirement. In the US distinguish programme admission/rotations/direct PI recruitment; elsewhere check institution-specific supervision procedures.
- Numbers supervised and completed, primary vs joint supervision, reliable completion durations, student publications, academic/industry/other destinations, funding and career support.
- A new PI without completed PhD graduates is not automatically weak; investigate co-supervision, postdoctoral mentoring, and available senior oversight.
- Current student load, postdoc/co-supervisor coverage, non-academic career support, and formal transfer/change-of-supervisor rules.

**Sources:** formal supervisor and admissions registers, graduate-school rules, ProQuest/university repositories, CNKI/Wanfang theses, lab alumni lists, credited student publications, public LinkedIn profiles, and student interviews.

**Minimum student-outcome table (verified data only):** `Degree stage | Student or anonymized aggregate | Researcher's supervision role | Verified completion year/duration | Major output | Publicly verified destination | Source`. Protect privacy.

**Warning signs:** independently substantiated patterns of non-routine departures, unusually long completion relative to the same programme, recurring authorship disputes, absent supervision feedback, refusal of reasonable independent conversations, or no credible training plan. Do not impose universal international time-to-degree thresholds. "ABD" does not mean the dissertation is finished but awaiting defense.

### Dimension 4 — Laboratory and team culture

**Assessment criteria:** collaboration vs competition, overlapping projects, work hours, weekend/holiday expectations, leave and sick leave, meetings, training, data stewardship, authorship discussions, safety, emotional conduct, third-party assistance channels, and context for turnover.

**Sources:** current/historical lab staff pages; current/former members; independent conversations across groups; institutional research-culture policies; on-site visits where available. Anonymous reviews are leads, not verified findings.

**Interview questions:** What is a normal week's workload? What happens after failed experiments? How are projects and authorship allocated? Can students take leave? Where can conflicts be escalated?

**Warning signs:** independently convergent accounts of coercive hours, bullying or threats, retaliatory authorship/resource decisions, major safety issues, or repeated unexplained departures. A busy experimental lab or staff turnover alone is not proof of mistreatment.

### Dimension 5 — Academic and professional reputation

**Assessment criteria:** verifiable peer recognition, professional-society/editorial/conference service, collaborations, integrity, student and peer experiences, and properly evidenced disputes.

**Sources:** In Mainland China, relevant supervisor-review sites, Yankong, Muchong, Zhihu, and other labs in the institution. In the other countries, PI Review, Rate My Professors, GradCafe, Reddit r/PhD, appropriate review sites, and independent departmental contacts. Verify current accessibility and sampling bias. **Rate My Professors mainly concerns course teaching, not doctoral mentorship.**

**Suggested question:** `Would you choose this lab again?` Follow up on strengths, difficulties, conflict resolution, and support for careers outside academia. Evaluate the specific answer and verifiable examples; do not psychologize response pauses.

**Warning signs:** multiple genuinely independent accounts of the same serious issue, or substantiated research/professional integrity proceedings. Do not repeat unverified personal accusations or identify private complainants.

### Dimension 6 — Education and professional background

**Assessment criteria:** documented degrees, postdoctoral training, appointment chronology, current title, independent PI authority, appointment duration/planned departure, teaching and administrative duties, lab maturity, actual supervision credentials, and nature of awards.

**Mainland China:** verify Academician status, Changjiang Scholar awards, Distinguished Young Scholars/Excellent Young Scientists programmes, professor/associate professor titles, and master's/doctoral supervision qualifications against official dated records. Honorifics are not direct evidence of mentoring.

**UK/US/Canada/Australia/NZ:** In US/Canada distinguish tenure-track, tenured, research, adjunct, and emeritus appointments plus actual supervision rights. In the UK interpret Lecturer, Senior Lecturer, Reader, and Professor in the local university's system. In Australia/NZ distinguish Lecturer, Senior Lecturer, Associate Professor, Professor, and research-fellow grades. `Fellow` may mean a job or an honor; IEEE Fellow, Fellow of the Royal Society, and HHMI Investigator are not interchangeable.

**Sources:** official profiles/CVs, ORCID, appointment notices, scholarly society records, doctoral theses, graduate policies.

**Warning signs:** impending departure without continuation arrangements, missing supervision authority, demonstrably insufficient availability, or inaccurate representations of titles. **Never infer performance or temperament from gender, age, or nationality.**

### Dimension 7 — Research agenda and applicant fit

**Assessment criteria:** coherent scientific questions, genuine novelty, technical programme, facilities and methods, plausible 3–5 year development, industrial/academic links, and available cross-disciplinary support.

**Applicant fit:** Compare confirmed applicant interests, wet-lab/computational skills, research experience, cross-disciplinary gaps, career objectives, and constraints against training offered. Classify as `Fit demonstrated / Skills to develop / Uncertain`. If personal data are missing, provide general conditions only.

**Sources:** recent representative papers, official project records, lab vacancies, advertised PhD projects, team competencies, and documented collaborations.

**Warning signs:** persistently vague plans, reliance on unattained essential funding/facilities, no training for crucial methods, or mismatch between applicant autonomy and project demands. Neither interdisciplinary movement nor institutional rank is an automatic negative.

**Outreach in the five English-speaking countries:** There is no universal rule that a candidate may contact only one potential supervisor per department. Check programme instructions; tailor messages, follow up reasonably, and avoid indiscriminate mass emails. Some US programmes admit centrally then rotate, whereas many other programmes encourage pre-application contact; official requirements control.

### Dimension 8 — Communication and accessibility

**Assessment criteria:** emails and meeting follow-through, regular one-to-one supervision, group meetings, manuscript-feedback turnaround, progress milestones, arrangements during absences, access to independent student conversations, and institutional dispute resolution.

**Sources:** published supervision agreements, postgraduate handbooks, pre-application contact, current/former student accounts, written project plans.

**Interview questions:** How often are individual meetings held? What is the typical paper-feedback turnaround? What would the first six months involve? Who supervises while the PI is away? May applicants speak privately with current and former students?

**Warning signs:** repeated unaddressed missed meetings, sustained lack of essential feedback, unclear supervision responsibility, evasiveness about funds or authorship, or obstruction of reasonable verification. **A single slow reply is not independent evidence of a problem.**
