# Project-quality and source-triangulation protocol (mandatory when judging a research project)

## Separate three objects of assessment

1. **Researcher's achievement**: established and attributed publications, discoveries, code, tools, grant leadership, translation.
2. **Current proposed project**: its scientific question, novelty, rigor, feasibility, resources, prospective student role, risk, timeline and training return.
3. **Supervision environment**: verified student outcomes and first-hand mentoring conditions. Neither 1 nor 2 proves 3.

## Project quality — minimum evaluation record

For every named project being recommended, record in the internal evidence log:

| Aspect | Test | Acceptable evidence | Uncertainty guard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Significance | Specific unsolved question and why it matters | Research proposal, recent field literature, formal award abstract | Topic's popularity is not scientific merit |
| Originality | What differs from existing studies | Prior art and explicit hypothesis | Never infer novelty from an attractive project title |
| Rigor | Controls, measurement, alternative explanations, replication | Protocol, preprint, research plan or article methods | A project listing rarely establishes methods quality |
| Feasibility | Essential datasets, instruments, skills, biological resources | Confirmed facilities, budget, feasibility/pilot work | Do not assume facilities are available to a named student |
| Funding and term | Award role, expiration, student's funding | Grant record and written admissions/scholarship terms | Historical PI award ≠ current funded slot |
| Student pathway | Concrete first 3–12 months, deliverables, ownership, authorship | Project plan/supervisor agreement/student interview | Master’s 60/90/120 points ≠ PhD vacancy |
| Resilience & skills | Plan B, transferable skills and employment relevance | Research methods and specific training commitments | Risk and reward depend on degree duration |

Assess project science through **importance, rigor/feasibility, and investigator/resources** as distinct questions, reflecting the applicable NIH simplified peer-review framework (https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/peer-review/simplifying-review/framework), without copying its grant-award scoring system into supervisor selection. Assess each as **Verified / Supported but incomplete / Unknown**. Add what question or document would resolve each unknown. Avoid total numeric project scores. A competitive award is a favorable peer-review signal, not proof of successful execution or supervision.

## Source channel strengths and limitations

- **Official appointments/programme policies**: good for current rank, degree routes and formal supervisor accreditation; weak for personal mentoring quality and real-time group funding.
- **Publisher versions/author contribution statements**: strong for paper claims, explicit author roles and reported methods; cannot directly establish an independently operated current lab.
- **Grant-award databases**: strong for award year, nominal value, declared PI and project goal; weak for remaining balances and student scholarships.
- **Scholar/Scopus/OpenAlex/CNKI/WoS**: useful for discovery, citations and works lists; can conflate names/versions and cannot establish research quality alone. Do not report database metrics without direct dated validation.
- **Theses, alumni pages, career profiles**: useful for specific public graduate trajectories; absence does not reveal dropout or completion rate.
- **Current/former student conversations**: most informative for lived supervision and lab culture, but can be selective and require corroboration. Without interviews mark not checked.
- **Reddit, Chinese forums, anonymous PI-rating platforms, video/social media**: candidate leads, not evidence of misconduct or true graduate outcomes. Separate teaching ratings from research supervision.
- **Media stories and university publicity**: reliable for what is publicly announced or quoted, not independent proof of realized translational outcomes.

## Synthesis without repetition

Keep all eight original dimensions in an **internal coverage map**. Put each unique fact in its dedicated named Academic CV topic section once; see `references/academic-cv-style.md`. The brief decision must offer three distinct conclusions: **researcher achievement/capability; proposed project quality and readiness; mentoring/fit**. Do not add a separately printed eight-dimension summary or reiterate bibliographic and grant details. Use short source labels for deep evidence and links at the end. Never request or add portraits.

## Acceptability gate

A project cannot be called **rigorously assessed** when only an advertisement/title exists. A PI's prior publications cannot substitute for a current funded project protocol. State the report's evidence level and identify missing independent student feedback explicitly.
