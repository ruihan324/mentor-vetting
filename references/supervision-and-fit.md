# Decision, admission, mentoring, and applicant fit

## 5. Cross-dimensional decision checks (not a ninth dimension)

### 5.1 Four separate questions: "Can this person take me?"

Check independently:

1. **Researcher's interest:** Are they intending to recruit this type of student/employee?
2. **Formal authority:** Can they act as primary/co-supervisor or hiring manager in the programme?
3. **Institutional eligibility:** Applicant's degree, language, research experience, selection/rotations, and deadlines.
4. **Funding and facilities:** Is a scholarship assured, an employment agreement secured, and the research infrastructure available?

One level does not establish the next. Without a direct response, **do not invent an admission probability**. State what the published information supports, contradicts, or cannot resolve, and give targeted follow-up questions.

### 5.2 Separate research from mentoring

- **Research capability:** originality, quality, continuity, independence, resources, representative outputs.
- **Supervision capability:** verified eligibility, past student outcomes, available time, career support, group management, and formal oversight.

Provide at least one evidence-based conclusion for each. Strong performance in one does not guarantee the other. For postdoc/collaboration/faculty applications, prioritize the relevant employment and research-independence issues without forcing PhD-specific metrics.

### 5.3 Generate targeted questions automatically

Before selecting questions, load `references/interviews-and-risk-signals.md`. Ask distinct stakeholder groups for observable events, written conditions and examples rather than invitations to diagnose a supervisor's personality. A question about a lab's practices does not imply the subject has committed misconduct.

Select **3–7 questions that could materially change the decision**, ordered by importance and identifying whom to ask (PI, graduate office, current student, or alumnus). Derive them from actual evidence gaps, not generic boilerplate. For PhD applications consider formal admissions, complete funding duration, project, meeting cadence, student completion. For postdocs consider terms and autonomy; for faculty roles appointment/promotion provisions.

### 5.4 Measure evidence completeness, not personality

Per dimension present:

`Main verified facts | Material contrary evidence/concerns | Missing evidence | Status`

You may report **8/8 dimensions attempted** and **N/8 dimensions with independently verified evidence**. Do not interpret low public-data availability as poor mentoring or calculate pseudo-objective quality scores or acceptance probabilities.

## 9. Final assessment and recommendations

### 9.1 Decision categories (not pseudo-precise scores)

- **High priority for initial contact:** substantial public evidence of suitability and no known major obstacles concerning eligibility or resources.
- **Worth further investigation:** appealing strengths plus important unknowns.
- **Proceed cautiously pending verification:** substantiated material risks or unresolved essential funding/contract/training terms.
- **Insufficient evidence for a reliable recommendation:** do not decide based on impressions.

If comparing multiple researchers, use consistent, justified criteria and acknowledge peer comparability; do not fabricate probabilities of acceptance or uncalibrated rankings. Screen eligibility, admission route and written funding first; compare scientific contribution, *current* project readiness and documented supervision separately. Do not use fixed numbers of students or generic PI career stages as universal red-flag cutoffs.

### 9.2 Every useful final judgment answers

1. **Who is this person?** Are identity, current appointment, and supervision rights established?
2. **What have they achieved scientifically?** What works, contributions, independence, resources, and continuity have actually been demonstrated?
3. **Can they supervise effectively?** What verified student outcomes, mentoring practices, and oversight are known?
4. **Are they suitable for the user's purpose?** Admissions/job fit, training, funding, and career trajectory.
5. **What crucial uncertainties remain?** Which answers would change the conclusion?
6. **What should be verified next, and with whom?** Suggest steps; do not make contact for the user.
