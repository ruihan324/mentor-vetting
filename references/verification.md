# Identity, evidence, and completeness gates

No publication, award, thesis, or supervision record may be assigned on name similarity alone. Tool-generated validation is a pre-screen and cannot establish scientific contribution or innocence of misconduct.

## Verify identity *before* counting papers or awards

Check, in order:

1. Current official university/department/laboratory profile and appointment dates.
2. Name variants, transliterations, former institutions, and research specialties.
3. ORCID, scholarly profile, and publisher author records with affiliations.
4. Institutional affiliations, co-authors, themes, and dates on papers, grants, and patents.
5. Archived institutional webpages if changes in appointment need resolving.

Require **an official identity source plus at least one independent supporting identifier**. Name similarity, topic similarity, or adjacent search results do not establish identity.

**Namesake isolation:** Record competing candidates by names, institutions, specialties, co-authors, publication dates, and ORCID IDs. Attribute each publication, award, grant, and patent individually. **Do not count an unverified item** in totals or representative achievements; label it "attribution not verified" in the research log. Pay special attention to institutional renaming, Chinese name transliteration, joint affiliations, and same-name researchers within a discipline.

Do not seek or embed personal portraits or generic avatar placeholders in the report.

## Evidence and research-assessment standards

### Evidence levels (not a rating of the researcher)

- **Level 1 — Directly verifiable fact:** official university records, grant-award databases, publisher/DOI records, formal policies, publicly accessible theses, authoritative corrections/retractions.
- **Level 2 — Corroborative information:** Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, OpenAlex, verifiable CVs, lab websites, public career profiles. Account for staleness, coverage, and namesakes.
- **Level 3 — Experiences and reputational leads:** *actual* first-hand interviews, second-hand reposts, anonymous forums, supervisor-review platforms, and social media are different provenance levels; retain those distinctions. Attribute claims to sources; seek independent confirmation. Never claim to have entered a blocked platform or contacted a student if not done.
- **Level 4 — Unsupported inference:** assumptions about competence or character from age, gender, nationality, ethnicity, appearance, one slow reply, social demographics, a single departure, or no publicly listed grant; attempts to decode supposed subtext in routine academic speech. Never present these as verified facts or negative verdicts.

For each consequential claim include **status** (`Verified / Partially supported / Needs confirmation / Not found / Not applicable`), **source link**, and **date checked**. Cross-check serious or disputed allegations against independent evidence. One anonymous accusation is not a confirmed conclusion. **"Not found" does not mean "does not exist."**

### Minimum evidence-log fields

For material claims, record as available:

`Claim | Primary-source URL | Issuing body | Publication/event date | Access date | Basis for researcher identification | Caveats/counterevidence | Relevant dimension`

Do not claim to have verified a specific record if only a database landing page was accessible. Clearly flag paywalls, inaccessible sites, incomplete search coverage, and unavailable databases.

### Data definitions and reporting windows

- Default to the **most recent five years** for publications and grants, emphasizing activity in the most recent three years. Include earlier truly influential works separately.
- State the source and retrieval date for citations, h-index, and i10-index. Do not add counts from separate databases; i10-index is principally a Google Scholar metric.
- Journal Impact Factor (JIF) describes a **journal**, not an individual paper. Do not confuse JCR quartiles with Chinese Academy of Sciences journal classifications. State the metric year.
- Avoid universal publication-count, JIF, or journal-quartile thresholds across fields, career stages, and experimental time scales.
- Count preprints, original peer-reviewed papers, conference outputs, reviews, patents, software, and datasets separately as appropriate.
- Verify first/corresponding/co-first/co-corresponding author labels, explicit contribution statements, and field-specific authorship conventions. A senior PI being first author occasionally is not itself a warning.
- Distinguish retractions, expressions of concern, and corrections; explain the documented reason before interpreting them.
- Do not calculate completion, dropout, or delay rates without a trustworthy admission denominator and outcome history. Thesis acknowledgments cannot prove supervision quality.
- Do not infer a PI's discretionary funds or a student's stipend from a grant's **total award value**.

### Three completion levels (display on the first page)

- **Level 1 — Preliminary public-information investigation:** identity reasonably supported; all eight dimensions attempted; selected outputs/projects located; key gaps remain in publication coverage, student outcomes, current grants, or other critical facts.
- **Level 2 — In-depth public-record verification:** identity established; deduplicated multi-database five-year review; substantive assessment of at least **three representative outputs** (or all if fewer exist); institutional funding and supervision status checked; unavailable public facts clearly identified. Do not claim this level unless the conditions are met.
- **Level 3 — Key training/resource facts verified:** Level 2 requirements plus direct, reliable confirmation of actual admission/supervision arrangements, relevant current funding or documented support, and student-training outcomes. If interviews or written confirmations are needed but unavailable, do not claim Level 3.

At every level report each dimension as **Verified / Partially supported / Not found / Needs confirmation**. **Eight dimensions covered does not mean eight dimensions verified.** Do not issue mechanical numerical scores for research quality or a predicted likelihood of admission.

### Risk-evidence thresholds

- **Established concern:** directly verified evidence of a problem relevant to the applicant's decision.
- **Concern requiring verification:** convergent indirect signals or important unresolved contract/training arrangements.
- **Insufficient information:** no defensible conclusion; do not penalize the subject for lack of public documentation.

Explain why a concern matters for **this particular applicant's goal**. Positive claims must also be evidenced. No unsupported 100-point scores, precise admission probabilities, or sensational labels.

## Research source directory

**Preferred order:** original official record > publisher/scholarly database > independently verifiable lab/institution records > public career profiles > anonymous discussion. Never imply that an inaccessible, subscription-only, or blocked page was personally reviewed.

### Mainland China

- **Identity and admissions:** universities, departments, graduate schools, current recruitment lists, partner institutions, official appointments.
- **Papers and graduates:** https://www.cnki.net/ | https://www.wanfangdata.com.cn/ | publisher pages, institutional theses.
- **Grants:** https://www.nsfc.gov.cn/ | https://www.letpub.com.cn/ (secondary lead), central/local agency award announcements.
- **Reputational leads:** public Xiaomuchong/Zhihu discussions or other accessible forums, if source authenticity can be checked; otherwise use only as an unverified lead.

### United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand

- **Researcher identity and outputs:** https://scholar.google.com/ | https://orcid.org/ | https://openalex.org/ | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ | Web of Science | Scopus | publisher pages | https://www.researchgate.net/ (supporting only).
- **Grants:** country-specific official databases in Section 6; prefer an individual award page over a database homepage.
- **Student outcomes:** ProQuest Dissertations & Theses, university repositories, lab alumni, https://www.linkedin.com/ (publicly available career information).
- **Reputational leads:** Reddit, GradCafe, publicly accessible student experiences and review platforms (where independently accessible and relevant); do not imply anonymous ratings are representative.

### Across all regions

- **Research integrity:** https://retractionwatch.com/ | https://pubpeer.com/ | publisher Crossmark/corrections.
- **Identity:** check DOI-linked institutional affiliations, authorship lists, co-authors, grant awardees, and grant numbers against original records.
