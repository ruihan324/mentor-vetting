# First-hand investigation, interview prompts, and cautious risk interpretation

This is an operational interview and risk-triage aid. Never simulate interviews or claim a supervisor was contacted when they were not. If a private interview occurred, record its date and the interviewee's role privately; obtain permission before attributing or quoting anyone. Do not require a public URL or reveal their identity. Do not insert the entire question list into reports; select only the **3–7 unresolved, decision-changing** questions.

## Ask four groups different questions

| Person/source | Interview questions tied to verifiable issues | Evidence limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Supervisor / PI** | Who provides hands-on training? How often are project and writing feedback meetings planned? What is the first 6–12-month plan and fallback? Who owns project/data/IP and sets author order? Is a real funded student position available for the *entire* degree? How are absences and supervisor departure handled? | Applicant interview is a **statement of intent**, not a contract or observed long-term behavior. Verify funding/admission through university. |
| **Current students** | What happened at the most recent meeting? How long did an example draft take to receive actionable feedback? Who taught the essential technique? What occurs after a failed experiment? Are weekend/leave expectations written and practiced? Who resolves disputes in the supervisory team? | Personal accounts; seek range of roles and avoid coercing students into private disclosures. |
| **Graduated or departed students** | What was the actual route from project start to thesis completion? What support was available during difficult phases, job search and transitions? If you could decide again, what would you verify earlier? | Different cohort/time period; no representative rate from a few accounts. |
| **Graduate office or programme administrator** | Is this person accredited in this degree? Which admission model applies? What is the *written* funding/fee arrangement? What are minimum meeting/progress requirements? How can students change supervision or escalate complaints? | Offices may not release private personnel or student complaint information; do not imply access to confidential files. |

## Evidence-sensitive warning signal protocol

1. **Established serious concerns:** confirmed official findings or reliable, specific public records of relevant wrongdoing. Check person identity, date, issuing authority, procedural status, outcome and any later corrections or appeals; factual attribution only. Do not treat a *pending allegation* as a finding or announce an automatic blanket prohibition on working with a person.
2. **Converging specific concerns requiring verification:** independent first-hand reports describing the same documented behavior; a confirmed unexpected funding termination; material inconsistencies in written terms; unexplained repeated changes to formal supervision. Attribute and seek primary corroboration, especially for potentially defamatory statements.
3. **Interview prompts, not verdicts:** difficulty privately reaching students, high group turnover without cohort data, delayed initial email, unusually eager recruitment, large or small lab, PI's seniority, a lab corporate connection, research-only work, administrative appointments, lack of published alumni page, or joining a topic outside the lab's historical area. Each may have benign explanations. Ask context rather than translating these into a diagnosis or a fixed score.
4. **Never use:** race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, age, disability, family status, demographic composition or stereotypes as risk predictors. No tables of supposedly dangerous Chinese/foreign/female/young/older mentors. No decoding canned phrases like 'read more papers' or 'interesting idea' into certain intent; language is context dependent. Do not manufacture ‘response within 24 h = good advisor’ thresholds or encourage after-hours email 'tests'.
5. **Rates require denominators:** count of available theses or negative posts cannot establish admission-cohort completion, delay, turnover, authorship fairness or misconduct prevalence. If cohort entry dates and outcomes are not reliably available, the percentage is **unknown**.
6. **Private information:** do not collect names of non-public complainants, private health information, immigration details, or confidential HR allegations. For researcher conduct, restrict to verified public actions and official outcomes; never contact third parties without the user's explicit request.

## How to interpret common anecdotes responsibly

| Reported observation | Proper interpretation | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| 'The PI is busy; a postdoc trains us.' | Could be a legitimate training arrangement or inadequate oversight. | Who is accountable? Is the postdoc trained and available? What does PI personally review? |
| 'They meet every week.' | Frequency alone doesn't measure feedback quality. | Was advice useful? Were specific decisions and next steps documented? |
| 'They did not reply for 10 days.' | Recruiting availability uncertain, not a character inference. | Is a follow-up appropriate under that programme's normal timeline? |
| 'Students are published with the PI.' | Proof of coauthorship only; neither fair nor unfair authorship is automatic. | Contribution statements, written authorship norms, named student roles. |
| 'A student left the group.' | One exit cannot establish a pattern or cause. | Institutional/graduate support process, more cohorts, voluntary vs involuntary if public. |
| 'The supervisor will change institutions.' | Potential continuity issue, not automatic disqualification. | Written transition plan, funding ownership, co-supervisor responsibilities. |
| 'The PI won't let me meet students alone.' | Worth asking about access and consent, not standalone proof of abuse. | Is a neutral programme office or alumni contact available? |
| 'The group collaborates with a company.' | May provide industry training or create IP conflicts. | Student work, remuneration/contract, publication rights, time allocation. |

## Distinguish individual supervisor, student project, and institution

- Formal supervisor accreditation does not verify individual mentoring outcomes.
- A published supervisory code gives **rights, responsibilities and questions to ask**; it does not establish the supervisor's personal compliance or violation.
- Student interview reports must not become population statistics or claims of a misconduct finding.
- If serious allegations cannot be responsibly substantiated, do not repeat identifying details in the user-facing report. Say that no defensible public conclusion can be drawn, then outline legitimate institutional verification routes.
